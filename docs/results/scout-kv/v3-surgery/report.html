scout_kv v3 real-data test: what makes real search hard

Real h is hard to search at L11, L15 and L43 because of how its components vary together within each token, and removing that joint structure also removes the body hubs. Shuffling each principal component on its own across the tokens of a block (s0), which keeps every component's values, the covariance and the block means, makes real search as easy as generated search at all three layers. Mapping every component to Gaussian quantiles by rank (gz), which removes heavy tails but keeps the joint structure, leaves L11 and L15 as hard as real and L43 partly so. Keeping the top 256 components intact (s256) is not enough at L11 and L15; at L43 it keeps most of the hardness.

Body hubness separates the outcomes. At each layer, every set that removes the hardness has lower body hubness than every set that keeps it or partly keeps it (L11: at most 11.9 against at least 20.1; L15: at most 18.0 against at least 35.7; L43: at most 7.2 against at least 32.3; real is 30.0–42.7). This split was read off the results, not fixed in advance.

The generators have body hubness 2.4–6.2 at these layers. v2 is easy at all three layers at both seeds (recall at least 0.862). v1 is easy at L11 and L15. At L43, v1 at seed 42 is inside the real range (0.617) with hubness 5.9, but v1 retrained at seed 1 is easy there (0.906, hubness 6.2), which the v1 diagnostics already put down to retrain noise. So one generated case reached real difficulty without hubs, and it did not reproduce. Body hubs are the measured property that tracks the hardness, so they are the candidate target for v3. The test shows that hubs and hardness go together, not that hubs alone cause it: the split was read after the run, and the transforms that remove hubs change other things too.

Hubness and search difficulty

Filled markers are the six sets at both seeds; open markers are v1 final and v2's two seeds at the same layers. The points fall into two groups. The low-hubness sets have recall L11 0.867 or more; L15 0.933 or more; L43 0.829 or more, and the others at most L11 0.622; L15 0.708; L43 0.800. The rank correlation over each layer's 12 set-seed points is L11 -0.45, L15 -0.67, L43 -0.91; it is weakest at L11, where the low-hubness sets' recall does not order with their hubness.

The test does not separate hubness from LID: the shuffles that remove the hardness also raise body LID by more than 3 at every layer (L11 real 17.5, s0 50.1). v1 and v2 separate them at L11, where their body LID (19.3 and 19.0) is within 2.5 of real (17.5) while their hubness is near 3 and their search is easy. So at L11, of these two properties, the generators miss the hubs and not the dimensionality.

Where queries land in v2's books

Real queries concentrate their top-10 keys on few keys at L7 and L11 (31 and 64); v2 spreads them over more than twice as many at both seeds. From L15 on the counts are similar, but real hub keys sit late in the books: their median position is above 25,000 at 8 of the 9 layers from L15 on (not at L39). At 8 of those 8 layers, v2's hub keys sit more than 1,000 positions earlier at both seeds.

What this means for v3

  • The candidate target is body hubs: a small set of tokens that many others have as near neighbours. Matching each component's distribution (heavy tails) or the top 256 components does not keep the hardness at L11 and L15.
  • Removing the within-token joint structure removes the hubs, so a loss or critic that sees whole tokens is needed to reward them; a per-component or low-rank target cannot.
  • Hubness skew is directly measurable on a batch, so it can be a training signal or a selection statistic, as LID and spectrum regularisers were for the earlier families.
  • Open: whether the components past rank 256 that matter at L11 and L15 are the directions W_k reads most; the key projection is not ordered by h variance.
