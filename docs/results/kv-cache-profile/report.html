How hard is real KV-cache search on Llama 4 Scout?

Query→key maximum-inner-product search on the 12 global (no-RoPE) attention layers of Llama-4-Scout-17B-16E-Instruct, measured on 4 PG-19 books × 12 layers × 8 key heads = 384 corpora of ~31.7k keys and 5,120 queries each. Measured 2026-10-16/17 on one A100-80GB.

Findings

  1. The out-of-distribution problem is a graph-index problem. HNSW reaches recall@10 ≥ 0.9 for real queries in 52.9% of corpora versus 100.0% for key-like queries, and at the paper's efSearch=176 the median recall@10 is 0.762 versus 1.000. Where HNSW does reach 0.9 everywhere, real queries cost a median 1.82× the distance computations (up to 12.6×).
  2. IVF shows no consistent gap. The per-head OOD÷ID scan ratio has median 1.15× with quartiles 0.56–2.46×, and recall at the operating point is 0.820 (OOD) versus 0.807 (ID).
  3. Removing the sink token is what makes the numbers stable. The document-to-document spread of IVF work fell from a median 3.78× (max 2789×) to 1.37× (max 5.3×). Sink-like heads remain even so: 34 corpora have ≤ 20 distinct keys across all queries' top-10 lists.
  4. Plain top-k is a poor attention estimate here; SoftmaxLift is much better, but only with exact search. Median attention error is 0.375 (top-k) versus 0.075 (SoftmaxLift), lower for lift in 384 of 384 corpora. Through approximate indexes the lift estimate degrades sharply in the late layers (Figure 5).
  5. Candidate target heads are in layers 39, 43 and 47, where the HNSW gap is largest and the heads are not sink-dominated. Layer 43 is the hardest.

Correction to the first run

The first profiling run reported that HNSW reaches recall@10 ≥ 0.9 for real queries in 16.7% of corpora versus 98.7% for key-side queries. That figure is withdrawn. It built HNSW directly on inner products, which graph indexes handle badly, and it kept the attention sink in the index. With the Xbox MIPS→L2 reduction and the sink removed (as in Zharkov et al. 2026) the figure is 52.9% versus 100.0%. The gap is real, but about a third as large as first reported.

Method

  • Model and capture. Llama-4-Scout-17B-16E-Instruct, unquantized bf16, all 50 shards verified against Meta's SHA-256 (downloaded from the ungated unsloth/ mirror; hashes identical). The attention input h was recorded at the 12 global layers 3, 7, 11, 15, 19, 23, 27, 31, 35, 39, 43, 47, which use no RoPE. query head j reads kv head j // 5. torch 2.14.1+cu130, transformers 4.57.6.
  • Generation recipe verified. At these layers k = Wkh and q = s(pos)·Wqh with s(pos) = 1 + 0.1·ln(1 + ⌊(pos+1)/8192⌋), recomputed from the checkpoint weights, reproduce what the model hands to attention to bf16 precision. Dropping s(pos) gives ≥ 9.5% error at positions ≥ 8191, so the check discriminates.
  • Corpora. First 32,768 tokens of 4 PG-19 test books. Keys: positions 1 … 31,743 (position 0, the sink, removed). Queries: the head group's 5 query heads at the last 1,024 positions (the recent window, not indexed). Real queries at position t would also see keys in [31,744, t); this simplification ignores them. ID baseline: keys at those last 1,024 positions.
  • Indexes. faiss 1.15.1. IVFFlat, inner product, 256 lists, nprobe swept 1–256; the operating point nprobe=24 probes the same 9.4% of lists as the paper's 96/1024 (a scaled equivalent, not their setting). HNSW on the Xbox reduction, M=48, efConstruction=500, efSearch swept 10–2048, operating point 176 (the paper's). Work at recall 0.9 is interpolated between the two bracketing sweep points.
  • SoftmaxLift. Keys (k, −log u), u ~ U(0,1] seeded per corpus; queries (q/√128, 1). Priority-sampling estimate from the returned K+1 = 128 keys. Attention error is over the indexed segment only.
  • Noise floor. Four documents is a small sample; Figure 3 is a lower bound on the real spread.
